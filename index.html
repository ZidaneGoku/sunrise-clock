What is this?

A clock built around the sun. Normal clocks stay fixed while sunrise wanders from about 5:50 AM in summer to 7:45 AM in winter (in Pittsburgh). This clock flips that around: the sunrise stays put at 6:00 AM every day, and the clock itself adjusts. If everyone woke at 6:00, they’d wake with the sun all year.

Version 1 keeps a normal 24-hour day. The clock reads 6:00:00 AM at every sunrise and runs a few seconds fast or slow per day so the next sunrise lands on 6:00 again. There are no jumps, and you’d never notice the difference.

Version 2 uses temporal hours, an ancient system from Egypt, Greece, Rome, and medieval Europe. Daylight is split into 12 equal day hours and the night into 12 night hours, so sunrise is always 6 AM and sunset is always 6 PM. In summer, day hours are longer than 60 real minutes, and in winter the night hours are.

Good to know: every location has its own sun-time, so this is a personal display, not a replacement for the clock you schedule meetings with. Near the poles, where the sun doesn’t rise or set for weeks, it shows “no cycle.” Your location is used only in your browser to do the math. It isn’t sent anywhere.

Sun positions use the NOAA solar algorithm. Accurate to about a minute.
